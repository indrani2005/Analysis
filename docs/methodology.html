1. Contribution statement

What this is not claiming. Empirical Bayes shrinkage, bootstrap ranking, and win-probability / leverage pressure are established tools in sports analytics. This submission does not invent them.

What this submission contributes. Their particular integration for SupeRR Selector: role-aware situational expectations, first-innings pressure modelling, within-competition normalisation (IPL vs T20I), and reliability-adjusted rankings that reward repeatable outperformance of context — not reputation or raw death-over averages.

Central question: when match conditions are genuinely difficult, does this player beat what a comparable player would be expected to produce in that situation — and can they do it again?

2. Problem framing

Raw strike rate and economy treat every delivery equally. A quiet powerplay single and a required-rate scramble in a live chase are not the same skill test.

We therefore (i) score pre-delivery match difficulty continuously, (ii) compare actual outcomes to role- and context-matched expectations, and (iii) rank by a bootstrap stability lower bound rather than a single peak performance.

4. Pressure definition

Pressure uses pre-delivery information only (no look-ahead).

Second innings

Rate stress (RRR−CRR & absolute RRR), resource stress (wickets & balls left), deadline stress, and closeness when the equation is tight.

First innings

Par deficit vs competition-family expected finish, wicket-collapse stress, and late-innings intensity. Pressure is not chase-only.

Live-contest filter

Hopeless late chases with extreme required rates are down-weighted so “pressure” means a live contest, not a decided game.

Leverage (conditional)

Match-blocked logistic WP leverage is blended in only after hold-out checks (AUC ≈ 0.80, Brier ≈ 0.18). Foundation remains the transparent resource/time score.

High-pressure balls are taken from the upper tail of the continuous score (~top 20% in the final freeze), with intensity weights above the cut.

5. Performance measurement

5.1 Role-aware expectations

Expected outcomes are cell means, shrunk with standard Empirical Bayes toward competition-family priors.

  • Batter cell: family × innings × phase × wicket band × role band (top / middle / lower) × RRR or par band.
  • Bowler cell: family × innings × phase × wicket band × pace/spin × RRR or par band.

Finishers are not judged against openers. Residuals credit runs above / prevented versus expectation, with a run-valued wicket term.

5.2 Absolute execution

Pressure strike rate (batters) and pressure economy / wicket rate (bowlers) are z-scored within competition among players with adequate samples. One-ball freak rates are excluded from the z pool.

5.3 Competition normalisation

IPL and T20I are normalised separately, then combined with sample-proportional weights. Single-competition players remain eligible; uncertainty is absorbed by the reliability layer. The Unstop shortlist additionally requires a meaningful IPL pressure sample so franchise-relevant evidence is not drowned by T20I-only volume.

6. Reliability-adjusted ranking

R = 0.35 · z(RAE) + 0.35 · z(Execution) + 0.05 · z(Clutchcapped) + 0.25 · f(Consistency)

Clutch (pressure residual − non-pressure residual) is lightly weighted and capped so always-good players are not punished for also performing in ordinary conditions. A par gate requires positive contextual residual under pressure.

Rstable = R̄bootstrap − σbootstrap

Primary sort key: match-cluster bootstrap lower bound (1 SD). Also reported: bootstrap mean, SD, and 90% lower bound.

Eligibility freeze: ≥ 200 pressure balls and ≥ 12 pressure matches (sensitivity also checked at lower thresholds).

8. Validation & limitations

Checks

Unit tests for extras/wickets/IDs; match-blocked WP gates; pressure sanity; eligibility & weighting sensitivity.

Limits

T20 pressure skill is noisy; venue/opposition effects are limited; par is family-based; rankings reflect this pack and these choices — not reputation.
